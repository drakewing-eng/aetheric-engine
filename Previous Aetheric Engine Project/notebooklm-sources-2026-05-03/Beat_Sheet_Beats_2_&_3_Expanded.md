### Beat 2 & 3 Expanded: The Sympathetic Circuit

**The Setting: Harrow’s Off-the-Grid Workspace**Rowan Ellis does not enter a haunted house; she breaches a closed thermodynamic system. The player’s first sensory input should not be visual, but acoustic and tactile. A persistent 1 985-cycle frequency vibrates through the audio track, immediately creating the sensation of pressure directly against the ribs [1].

**The Investigation Phase ("Poking Around")**As the player takes control of Ellis, the investigation is clinical and forensic. The room smells continuously of vaporized copper, heated varnish, and a faint, incongruous trace of lilac and ozone [2, 3]. Ellis uses her modern tools—a handheld SQUID magnetometer and digital audio recorders—to scan the room.

The player examines the physical degradation of the modern apartment:

The drywall is warped and softened, mimicking the structural decay of the oak floorboards at the 1856 Deveraux Estate [4, 5].

The floorboards are radiating heat [6].

Scattered on the desk are 19th-century folios where the iron-gall ink is blooming into circular halos under the sheer heat of the ambient current [7].

**The Discovery of Harrow (The "Biological Wire")**Ellis finds Dr. Harrow sitting in the center of the room, but he is not dead. He is locked in a Ficinian Trance. The player’s scanner reveals that Harrow is acting as the "Organic Resonator"—his nervous system has been "over-volted" by the sheer magnitude of the field, actively conducting the frequency [8].

When the player interacts with Harrow, Ellis applies her cold, institutional skepticism. She notes his shallow breathing, severe muscular tremors, and cardiac arrhythmia, diagnosing his state not as possession, but as the physiological consequence of acute galvanic shock and prolonged ozone toxicity [9].

**The Engine Revealed & The Interaction Mechanic**Looming behind Harrow is his reconstructed Aetheric Engine. It is a terrifying hybrid of Victorian architecture and modern technology: massive copper coils and a central brass horn, threaded through with modern fiber-optics and glass veins showing faint hairlines of thermal stress [10, 11]. The light within the glass does not simply shine; it turns in upon itself, spiraling inward with a flawless, suffocating geometry—the Ginzburgian Spiral [1].

It is here the player figures out the core gameplay loop. Ellis finds a note Harrow left behind, detailing his Whiteheadian panpsychist theories: he realized the 1856 Engine did not fail, but established a "relational condition" [12]. Harrow's notes reveal that the archive itself is not a record, but a circuit. He writes that the documentation itself has become a sympathetic resonator [13].

**The Gameplay Begins**To interact with the machine and attempt to break the Impedance Mismatch currently burning out Harrow's nervous system, Ellis must read the 1856 documents [8]. The game mechanics involve analyzing the texts (letters, lab logs, journals) and combining them.

When Ellis reads Ignatius Bell’s recovered final memorandum or Elspeth's diary aloud, the audio software registers the field shifting. The Engine responds to the cadence of the reading with vowel formants—a-o-a—vibrating directly into the player's teeth [14, 15]. The player must use the archive to strategically introduce "Euron" (the kinetic, disruptive counter-resonance of Grace) to shatter the suffocating "Law" of Uriel's field [16].

### The Editorial Breakdown for Gameplay:

**Forensics Over Frights:** The player’s primary actions are measuring magnetic drift, observing the physical degradation of ink and glass, and reading texts. The horror comes from the realization that doing the research powers the monster.

**The "Spectral Deepfake":** There are no transparent ghosts. The entity (Uriel) is experienced solely as a "Predatory Prehension" bending the air of the apartment into a crushing vacuum, mimicking the 1856 resonance to find a new biological host [8, 16].

**Ellis's Voice:** Throughout this sequence, Ellis’s internal monologue (and her notes) remain fiercely skeptical. She continually tries to debunk the horror using 21st-century physics, creating a brilliant cognitive dissonance as the player watches the Ginzburgian spiral literally alter the room's topology.
