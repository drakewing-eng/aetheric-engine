# Visual Novel Script Draft 1

### Visual Novel Script Draft 1

Note: this is not the same story as the Aetheric Engine/Archive novel. This is like the animated gameplay sequel.

### Movement I — The Archive (animated prelude)

The screen is dark. A faint harmonic tone begins, something like a tuning fork or glass resonance. Old paper textures appear slowly—letters, diagrams, wax cylinders, fragments of handwriting.

Rowan Ellis narrates over it, but not as someone telling a story. She sounds like someone explaining a manuscript she’s been studying for months.

She might begin something like:

“Most archives begin quietly. A box of letters. A ledger. Something misfiled in a provincial collection. The Richmond House archive did not begin quietly.”

Images fade in:

Victorian instruments.

A copper apparatus.

A candle flame trembling in a glass tube.

She continues explaining what the archive contains:

In 1856, a small group of researchers and patrons gathered at Richmond House to conduct an experiment. Their goal was not electricity, not magnetism, but something they called **harmonic sympathy**—the belief that light, breath, and vibration might reveal a deeper structure of reality.

As she speaks, fragments of the characters appear:

Bell writing in a laboratory notebook.

Selina in a prayerful pose beside the apparatus.

Rooke adjusting mechanical components.

Elspeth watching quietly.

The narration becomes slightly more uneasy:

“Their records suggest the experiment succeeded. Not in the way they expected.”

The apparatus vibrates.

Light bends.

The room darkens.

Rowan pauses briefly.

“After the final experiment, Richmond House was abandoned. The witnesses dispersed. Some recanted their testimony. Others refused to speak of the event again.”

Images shift to scattered documents being packed into boxes, sealed away.

“The archive survived only as fragments—letters, diaries, technical memoranda—rediscovered more than a century later.”

Then the tone shifts.

“One historian spent years assembling those fragments into a coherent record.”

A name appears on screen:

**Dr. Jonathan Harrow**

“He believed the archive preserved more than a historical curiosity.”

The animation briefly shows Harrow studying the documents, drawing connections between them.

“He believed it described a pattern.”

The tone drops slightly.

“And patterns can be repeated.”

The harmonic tone intensifies.

The animation ends with the apparatus diagram briefly overlaying the documents—almost as if the archive itself forms the shape of the machine.

Cut to black.

### Movement II — Rowan Ellis (present day)

The tone changes immediately. No dramatic music—just a quiet room.

We see a modern desk. Stacks of papers. A laptop. Draft pages of **The Aetheric Archive — Edited by Jonathan Harrow**.

Rowan speaks again, but now it’s conversational.

“That’s the story we tell in the introduction.”

She pauses, almost embarrassed.

“It’s a good introduction.”

A page of the manuscript is visible with editorial marks.

“But it isn’t the whole story.”

She explains her role briefly.

She wasn’t the historian who discovered the archive. She was brought in later to help prepare Harrow’s edition for publication—organizing the documents, checking references, writing editorial notes.

“At first I thought my job was simple. Clean up the manuscript. Put the documents in order. Make sure the citations existed.”

A pause.

“One of them didn’t.”

She flips through pages of the manuscript.

“Harrow references a memorandum written by Professor Bell. Several times. It’s important—maybe the most important document in the archive.”

But it isn’t in the files.

At first she assumes Harrow misplaced it. She tries contacting him.

No response.

She shows the manuscript to her supervisor.

They’re behind schedule. The press wants the book finished before the end of the year. Harrow hasn’t answered messages in weeks.

“They told me to mark the document as missing and move on.”

She sighs slightly.

“I tried.”

But the more she worked on the manuscript, the more she noticed something else: Harrow’s later notes weren’t written like historical commentary anymore.

“They read like observations.”

Like someone running an experiment.

That realization unsettles her.

“Harrow didn’t just study the archive. I think he tried to repeat it.”

Silence for a moment.

“So before the book goes to print, I’m going to find him.”

A small smile, a little self-aware.

“My supervisor thinks I’m chasing a misplaced document.”

She gathers a folder of Harrow’s notes.

“Maybe I am.”

She stands up.

“But if the document exists… I want to see it before anyone else does.”

The scene fades out.

### Player engagement point

After that scene, the player begins the VN.

The first playable sequence could be Ellis arriving at the place where Harrow was working—his lab, office, or rented workspace.

The player’s first task isn’t supernatural. It’s editorial:

**search for the missing memorandum.**

The player begins examining Harrow’s materials, just as Ellis would.

And that’s when the mystery truly begins.
