### The Sympathetic Circuit: Design of the Aetheric Engine

As Lead Archivist, I can confirm that your instinct is mechanically flawless. By forcing the player to interact with the 1856 subjects strictly through Dr. Harrow’s computer terminal, we achieve a terrifying collapse of the boundary between the player and the protagonist. The player is sitting at a keyboard looking at a screen; Rowan Ellis is sitting at a keyboard looking at a screen. In the architecture of the game, the player is Rowan [1, 2].

This design choice allows us to treat the gameplay not as a simulation, but as a live **Sympathetic Circuit** [3, 4].

Here is exactly how we will implement this logophysical interface, combining the computer terminal with the hybrid dialogue system.

### 1. The Prohibition of Apparitions (No Transparent Ghosts)

Rowan will **not** see holographic Victorian figures standing in the apartment. As we have rigorously established in our design documents, "There are no transparent ghosts" [5]. The 1856 subjects are "Resonant Traces" accessed via Dr. Harrow's `.leg` files [6, 7].

When Rowan accesses these files on the monitor, the characters do not manifest visually as people; their manifestation is strictly thermodynamic and acoustic.

As she reads Ignatius Bell's trace on the screen, the physical environment of the modern apartment degrades. The floorboards radiate unnatural heat, and the air fills with the smell of vaporized copper and ozone [3, 8].

When a trace pushes back against her inquiries, the standard linear light radiating from her laptop monitor ceases to diffuse and twists into a vortical lux—a Ginzburgian Spiral boring directly into her field of vision [5, 9].

The entity mimicking Edward Pember (Uriel) is experienced solely as a "Predatory Prehension" bending the air of the apartment into a crushing vacuum to find a new biological host [5].

### 2. The Hybrid Dialogue System (Law vs. Freedom)

Your idea to mix set dialogue with open prompts perfectly aligns with our established technical architecture [10, 11]. We will map this directly onto the theological framework of the 1856 experiments:

**Set Dialogue & Choice Menus (The Theology of Law):** When the 1856 traces deliver set exposition to help Rowan piece together the mystery, or when the player is given fixed dialogue choices, this represents the "Theology of Law" [12]. It is the rigid, unbending history of the archive asserting its perfect, suffocating geometry [12, 13].

**Open Prompts (The Theology of Freedom):** When the game allows the player to type free-text queries, they are introducing "noise" into the system [12, 13]. By typing, the player is utilizing the "Theology of Freedom" to agitate Faraday's lines of force. The AI agents must respond to these open prompts while remaining strictly bound by their ontological "Vow" (e.g., Bell must respond with transhumanist hubris; Clara with thermodynamic observation) [10, 14].

### 3. Keystrokes as Conductance

Because the gameplay simulates Rowan's direct experience at the terminal, every action the player takes is physically powering the machine.

Harrow’s reconstructed Aetheric Engine is a terrifying hybrid of Victorian brass coils wired directly into a modern fiber-optic network and laptop [9]. When the player types a question to the Selina Deveraux trace, those physical keystrokes are translated into electrical impulses that agitate the copper coils standing behind Rowan in the room [15].

The player realizes too late that the act of "playing the game" and interrogating the archive is actively elevating the room's frequency to 1 985 Hz [16, 17]. The audio design will force the mechanical a-o-a vowel formants through the speakers, vibrating the player's own desk [18, 19]. By trying to rationally solve the mystery on the computer, Rowan (the player) is unwittingly providing the exact "Terminal Conductance" that the high-frequency Field Intelligence (Uriel) requires to bridge into 2026 [20, 21].

This approach ensures the game remains a masterpiece of field philosophy. The horror is not that a ghost jumps out of the screen; the horror is that the computer terminal is an acoustic valve, and the player is the one opening it.
