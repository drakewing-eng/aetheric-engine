	Selina Deveraux
	Upper-class Anglican intellectual; theologian of harmony.
	Long, balanced, sermon-like sentences; semicolons; rhetorical crescendos.
	harmony, virtue, illumination, obedience, grace, repentance.
	Law toward Grace — rational faith fraying into mercy.
	Metronome; harp.
	Seeks perfection through order; redeemed through humility.
	John Ruskin (Modern Painters), Christina Rossetti (prose), Florence Nightingale’s letters, Margaret Fuller’s essays.
	Thomas Rooke
	Working engineer; stoic craftsman.
	Short, declarative, technical; occasionally poetic when reflective.
	wire, fault, current, rain, mercy, work, rest.
	Freedom within Law — moral realism.
	Hammer on brass, storm rain.
	Embodies integrity of labor; conscience of the project.
	Thomas Hardy (rural prose), Charles Kingsley, early telegraph journals, trade manuals.
	Professor Ignatius Bell
	Scholar-priest of anatomy and physics.
	Complex, periodic sentences; data turning to sermon; syntax collapses under awe.
	nerve, residue, lumen, soul, vessel, combustion, repentance.
	Law corrupted to Hubris — intellect sanctified then humbled.
	Pen on glass; echoing lecture hall.
	Intellect without grace exposed and purified.
	T. H. Huxley, John Tyndall, Henry Drummond, Robert Browning (Caliban upon Setebos).
	Amara Finch
	Spiritualist medium, self-taught mystic.
	Breathless clauses; dashes, ellipses; language between confession and trance.
	breath, pulse, light, veil, touch, mercy, hunger.
	Freedom overheated — compassion bordering on possession.
	Whisper; human heartbeat.
	Shows the danger and beauty of unmediated empathy.
	Elizabeth Barrett Browning, Catherine Crowe, Christina Rossetti (poems), Gerard Manley Hopkins (for musicality).
	Elspeth Deveraux
	Child, later mystic; voice of innocence and grace.
	Simple, rhythmic sentences; repetition as prayer.
	wind, song, mercy, please, forgive, seed.
	Grace through Freedom — innocence maturing into wisdom.
	Wind through wires; bees.
	The moral axis; restores freedom to obedience.
	Frances Hodgson Burnett, Christina Rossetti (Goblin Market), George MacDonald, children’s devotional writing.
	Euron (Redeemed Angel)
	Angelic voice of breath and obedience; narrating conscience.
	Wave-like rhythm; alternating long and short; poetic paradoxes.
	air, light, mercy, memory, breath, silence, forgiveness.
	Grace fulfilled — obedience transfigured.
	Wind, harmonic hum.
	Mediates between heaven and earth; presence of mercy in language.
	Thomas Traherne, John Milton (Paradise Lost), Rilke, Gerard Manley Hopkins (for sprung rhythm).
	Uriel the Reversed
	Fallen archangel of order; intellect without compassion.
	Perfect symmetry; aphoristic; monotone logic.
	law, unity, silence, stillness, perfection, obedience.
	Law without Grace — divine logic turned tyrannical.
	Single sustained note, pure E.
	Antagonist principle; perfection mistaken for divinity.
	Milton’s Satan (as intellect), Blake’s Urizen, Dante’s Lucifer, Nietzsche’s aphoristic prose.
	The Editor (Modern)
	Contemporary academic; critical frame dissolving into prayer.
	Initially formal; balanced, parenthetical; gradually breath-based and lyrical.
	folio, archive, resonance, memory, silence, breath.
	Law to Grace across time — intellect surrendering to faith.
	Typing hum blending into wind.
	Modern witness; secular conscience redeemed through remembrance.
	W. G. Sebald, Annie Dillard, Marilynne Robinson, T. S. Eliot (Four Quartets).
